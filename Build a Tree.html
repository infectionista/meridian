Build a tree by clicking

Find the smallest number in the table and click it. Those two join, a fork appears on the tree, and every number that changes is recalculated as a simple average — shown step by step below.

How it works

01

Every number in the table is a count: how many letters differ between those two. Hover a number, or tab onto it, to see exactly which letters.

02

Click the smallest number. The closest pair joins first, and the fork on the tree sits at that distance.

03

After a join, the table gets a fresh row of numbers: the distances from the newly joined pair to each of the others. Every new distance is just an average of numbers that were already in the table — hover it to see which old numbers went into it.

    Two checks on your own answer

    Rule one

    Your join distances must always increase. If a join comes out lower than the one before it, the join order is wrong — go back.

    Rule two

    No distance can be larger than the number of letters compared. If it is, the counting went wrong somewhere.

    Average linkage (UPGMA). Stage 1 compares surnames letter by letter; stages 2–3 compare short aligned fragments of one shared gene (small-subunit rRNA) — not whole genomes — in exactly the same way.
